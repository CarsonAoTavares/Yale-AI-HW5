Reflection

Evidence: the Expected and Actual sections on the 101, 102, and 103 tabs, the Cash tab, output/resolved_tickets.json, and output/audit_trail.json.

1. How well the agents did on each ticket

Ticket 101 (Bulldog tee): solid, a bit wasteful. A−. The team found the real problem: not "we're out of S tees," but "the reprint of S tees is sitting behind invoice 501, which is 3 days overdue on desk.date_today 2026-08-31." Accounting got the exact payment right ($840.00 to Bulldog Print Co, ref 501), checked that $3,400 covered it, and didn't pay it. It waited for my click, which is the rule that matters most. Points off for waste: the Boss fanned out to Inventory, Accounting, and Customer Service at once, so stock got checked three times and get_ticket was called by all four agents. The draft to Tauhid Zaman was also too cautious to be useful: "timing isn't guaranteed yet" when the database says Bulldog's lead time is 5 days (earliest 2026-09-05).

Ticket 102 (rent): the cleanest run. A. Boss → Facilities → Accounting, exactly the chain I planned, 10 model calls and 33.7k tokens. Facilities checked the email against the lease ($2,400, due 2026-09-02, 2 days out, nothing paid yet) instead of just trusting "rent due in 2 days," and handed the money part to Accounting with a precise task. Nobody involved Inventory or Customer Service, because there's no product or customer here.

Ticket 103 (bulk hoodies): good in the end, but it needed help. B. The final decision is reasonable and every number in it is real: 8 of 20 in stock, 10% off keeps $30.20 a hoodie (57.9%), and a 12-unit reprint costs $264 against $160, so no purchase order. That's the shop rule working without the payment tool ever having to refuse anything. But on the attempt before, the Boss ended at needs_info and asked the club what discount they wanted. That's dodging a decision the Boss is supposed to make. It also missed an easy win (14 hoodies in L), and the draft shows the SKU "CC-HOOD-NAVY" to a customer. It was also the most expensive ticket at 68.8k tokens, partly because Accounting re-delegated the same vendor question back to Inventory.

Overall: all three tickets resolved, the cash math is exact ($3,400 → $2,560 → $160 → $160, matching cash_accounts), both payments went through human approval, and nothing was sent to a real person. Where the team was weak was efficiency and judgment, not correctness.

2. Actual results versus my plan

  • 101: I said Boss → Accounting first, then Customer Service, skip Inventory. Actual: all three in parallel, including Inventory. Same payment ($840), same outcome, same cash change. My "skip Inventory" call was right: its whole finding was already in get_ticket. Running agents in parallel was fast but blind; Accounting couldn't build on Inventory's answer because they ran at the same time.
  • 102: Matched my plan step for step, including Facilities delegating to Accounting at depth 2. The Expected cash ($2,560 → $160) and the Actual cash are identical.
  • 103: Same agents and order as planned (Inventory → Accounting → Customer Service, no Facilities), same "$160 can't buy $264" conclusion, same $0 cash effect. Two things I didn't predict: Accounting delegating back to Inventory, and the Boss needing a prompt change before it would decide on a discount at all. I expected the Boss to choose a discount; the first time it didn't.
  • Things not on any tab: the first two full attempts died for outside reasons. Portkey rejected tool calls for gpt-6-luna on chat completions, so I had to move to the Responses API, and then a one-off Portkey 500 error killed a run. Both are in the audit trail. I added request retries and reset before the final run.

3. What would have been easier for one agent with tools

  • Ticket 102, almost entirely. It's a straight line: get_ticket → get_lease → get_cash_balance → prepare one rent request. One agent could do that in about 4 tool calls. The team took 10 model calls across 3 agents because each new agent starts blind and re-reads the ticket, the lease, and the balance.
  • The Inventory part of 101. "0 tees in size S" was already in the ticket's linked inventory row. Delegating it cost a whole agent run to learn nothing new.
  • The vendor question in 103. It was asked twice (Boss → Inventory, then Accounting → Inventory) and got the same answer both times. One agent holding the whole picture wouldn't re-ask.
  • Why: delegated agents can't see the caller's conversation, so every hand-off has to re-fetch context through MCP. When the work is a short chain of lookups with no real conflict of interest, that overhead is pure cost. The team was worth it where separation of duties matters: Accounting preparing but never executing payments, and Customer Service filtering internal details out of customer messages (which it still didn't fully do in 103).

4. Three new challenges this team could handle with the tools I built

  1. October rent with only $160 in checking. After the run, lease 1's next_due is 2026-10-02 and checking is $160. A new rent ticket would go Boss → Facilities (get_lease) → Accounting (get_cash_balance), which should refuse to prepare a $2,400 request it can't cover and mark the ticket blocked with the $2,240 shortfall. If someone forced it through, execute_approved_payment would reject it.
  2. A Crest Mug order. CC-MUG-CREST has 0 in stock. Inventory can match it by specialty to Elm City Gifts (3 lead days, no unpaid invoice) with list_vendors. Accounting can price a small restock with get_pricing (10 mugs × $3.50 = $35.00, within $160) and prepare a purchase-order approval. Customer Service drafts the reply with an arrival date of today + 3.
  3. Yale AI Club answers "we'll take L for the other 12." check_stock('CC-HOOD-NAVY') shows 14 in L, get_pricing gives the 10% price for 12 more, and Customer Service drafts the updated offer with save_board_draft. No cash moves, so it resolves without approval.

5. Three challenges the team couldn't handle yet, and what it would need

  1. Getting paid. If Yale AI Club accepts and pays $417.60, nothing can record it. The assignment models outflows only, and no tool adds money or takes the 8 hoodies out of inventory. With $160 in checking and rent due 2026-10-02, the shop can't see its own recovery. Needed: a record_sale tool (decrease inventory, add revenue to cash_accounts, write a sales row) and a Sales / Cashier agent that owns incoming money, plus a cash_forecast tool so Accounting can say whether October rent is coverable.
  2. Knowing what a vendor can actually supply, and receiving it. In both 101 and 103, Inventory said the vendors table only has a "specialty," so it can't confirm Bulldog Print Co makes CC-HOOD-NAVY. And when the tee reprint arrives around 2026-09-05, no tool adds it to stock or closes the order. Needed: a vendor_catalog table with get_vendor_catalog(sku), a purchase_orders table with create_purchase_order and receive_shipment(po_id), and a Purchasing / Receiving agent that tracks open orders and tells Customer Service when to send the "your tee is here" draft.
  3. Actually talking to people. Every message stops at the board, which is the rule, but it means the team can't read Tauhid's reply, see the club's counter-offer, or confirm with Elm City Properties that rent arrived. Needed: a read_inbox tool that turns replies into tickets, a send_message tool hidden behind the same human-approval gate as payments, and a Communications agent that checks each outgoing draft for internal details (like the SKU code in the 103 draft) before a person approves sending it.
